**6/10 as written.** The monster theme and outdoor social play are memorable, but I would not currently see a clear “Touch Grass” winner. The design’s strongest track fit—putting the phone away—is undermined by its most ambitious mechanic. A focused version is feasible in 30 hours; the full specification is not a credible commitment, even with AI generating most code.

Prioritized findings:

1. **Make being away from the screen the main experience.** Six-second attacks, navigation instructions, pickups, and photo races keep attention on the phone. **Change:** make cooperative Hunt the core: receive one prompt, pocket the phone for several minutes, stop to photograph, then regroup. Use successful discoveries to damage Krillion and unlock a shared finale. Demonstrate actual screen-off time to distinguish it from a generic AI scavenger hunt.

2. **Cut scope before implementation.** Three modes, background behavior, two mobile platforms, GPS combat, AI judging, and a spectator client create too many integration boundaries. **Change:** ship one five-minute session, 2–4 players, 10–15 curated prompts, one shared boss-health mechanic, and one results screen. Cut streaks, evening nudges, steps, generated prompts, sweeping tides, pickups, and GPS revives. If both original ideas must survive, make Dodge one optional, slow round between hunts; cut simultaneous Siege.

3. **The escape guarantee is mathematically unsupported.** At 1.4 m/s, a player walks only 8.4–16.8 m during a 6–12 second warning. Escaping from the centre of a 15–30 m strike with a 5 m margin takes approximately 14–25 seconds, before reaction time. **Change:** calculate deadlines from the actual escape distance plus reaction, communication, and location uncertainty—or remove timed physical dodging.

4. **A circular arena is not a safe playing area.** It can contain roads, buildings, fences, stairs, and inaccessible exits. Overlapping strikes can remove otherwise valid escapes. “Stay off roads” does not fix generation. **Change:** restrict any Dodge trial to an inspected, open field with an inset playable boundary; allow one hazard at a time and an immediate pause. Remove first-photo speed bonuses and instructions to run. Provide a walking pace.

5. **GPS uncertainty cannot support the proposed precision reliably.** `radius - accuracy/2` is an arbitrary fairness rule; with a 15 m radius and 30 m accuracy, nobody can be hit. An 8 m revive also depends on errors from *two* phones. **Change:** use broad, forgiving zones, timestamp every fix, suspend penalties on stale or poor fixes, and require sustained presence for arrivals. Replace proximity revives with teammate confirmation. Requested update intervals are not a guaranteed cross-platform clock. [Expo Location](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/location/)

6. **The locked-screen promise needs a separate technical plan.** Foreground location and an active WebSocket do not establish reliable background gameplay. Expo Go also lacks background-location support. **Change:** make Hunt work from a prompt and deadline downloaded before locking, with a pre-scheduled local notification and server resync on return. Test that exact flow immediately on real devices. Any background combat requires a development build and additional lifecycle work. [Expo Location](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/location/)

7. **“Server authoritative” is underspecified.** A 2 Hz broadcast rate is reasonable; it does not define correct deadlines, disconnect handling, or duplicate scoring. **Change:** specify server-owned round IDs and deadlines, reconnect tokens, full-state resync, idempotent submissions, and one score award per player/prompt. Pause combat penalties when location becomes stale. Reserve the final 8–10 hours for device integration and rehearsal.

8. **The indoor demo could prove only the simulator.** A joystick and bots demonstrate software, but cannot establish successful outdoor play. **Change:** capture a short outdoor clip showing two people pocketing phones and completing a round. At the table, let judges photograph a prepared object. For the stage: 20 seconds explaining the loop, 20 seconds outdoor evidence, 50 seconds live photo/verdict/shared boss damage, 30 seconds results. Keep simulation visibly labelled and a recorded fallback ready.

9. **AI scoring needs predictable rules and failure handling.** Creativity scores are subjective; model response time should not decide “first.” Hash caching must include the prompt and judging version. **Change:** use binary validation with fixed points, server receipt time, schema validation, a timeout/retry path, and a verified image-capable model accessible with your key. Treat screen-photo rejection as best effort. [Gemini model catalog](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models)

10. **Distribution and privacy need explicit decisions.** External TestFlight testing can require review; `/watch/CODE` exposes live locations and photos behind a short code. **Change:** demo from already-installed, tested builds; make external TestFlight availability optional. Use a separate spectator token, consent before projecting photos, and expire room data. Remove prompts involving strangers and omit unmeasured steps or activity claims from the Grass Report. [Apple TestFlight review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/test-a-beta-version/invite-external-testers/)
