Most impactful remaining problems, in order:

1. **Play — direction is subordinate to distance.** “Walk 9 m” dominates while “south” is much smaller; the directional arrow is confined to the radar. Restore the previous version’s large arrow inside the instruction card, with direction and distance together. Give countdown numbers a seconds suffix.

2. **Results — the outcome looks contradictory.** “You outlasted the storm” follows a down state; “1 of 1 still standing” appears above three checked crew members. Make the headline explicitly describe the crew’s victory, show the actual standing/total count, and distinguish survivors from downed players in the roster.

3. **Play and big screen — essential radar details are too faint.** Rings, bearings, health dots and player labels are visually delicate for sunlight or projection. Increase label size and stroke weight, darken navigation marks, and strengthen the player marker’s outline. Keep decorative background contours quieter.

4. **Play — symbols lack a readable purpose.** Diamonds, circled crosses and the large black circular control introduce several unexplained visual languages. Use recognizable pickup/revive symbols; show the joystick only in demo mode and give it a visible thumbstick treatment.

5. **Clear/down states — the next action is ambiguous.** “Probe 30 m south” introduces unexplained terminology; “Nimbus is 27 m south-east” doesn’t establish who should move. Use the established walk instruction format when movement is required. In the down state, highlight your location and the teammate who can revive you.

6. **Lobby — small, pale controls undermine the otherwise strong hierarchy.** Duration/zone choices and “New big-screen link” are harder to read than the primary buttons. Increase option text size and contrast; consolidate projector setup into one clear control.

7. **Landing pages — repeated radar imagery demonstrates the instrument more than the game.** Replace one repeated radar illustration with an actual play screen showing a storm, movement arrow and countdown. It would explain Squall’s distinctive interaction faster than another paragraph or feature card.
