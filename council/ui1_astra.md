Most impactful remaining problems, in priority order:

1. **Round reveal / squad results — the reward is visually underplayed.** The score and rarity are tiny inside a relatively small card, surrounded by empty space. Enlarge and center the revealed card; show earned points prominently beside the tier. On results, make the winner and total score the headline.

2. **Collection / card detail / today — essential information becomes microprint.** Counts, points, rarity labels and monospaced metadata are difficult to read at the shown sizes, especially for outdoor use. Use readable UI text outside miniature cards; reserve card thumbnails for the photo, title and tier. Remove the unexplained “rarity 98.”

3. **Home / today — the unfinished task lacks emphasis.** Home prioritizes “245”; today gives the remaining prompt less visual weight than completed finds. Put “A pattern made of squares” directly above the home capture action, and make that unfinished row the clear primary action on today.

4. **Squad lobby — inviting friends is hidden behind a generic share icon.** The code dominates, but the action needed to populate the lobby is visually secondary. Make the code tappable to copy and place a labeled **Invite friends** button immediately beneath it.

5. **Home / lobby / hunt / landing — primary-action color changes arbitrarily.** Playing and capturing alternate between black and orange buttons. Standardize orange for the main forward action and outlined or text treatments for secondary actions.

6. **Mobile landing — the card fan sacrifices legibility and delays the pitch.** Cards clip at the viewport edges, with cramped titles and wrapping prompt labels. Use three larger, deliberately overlapping cards; tighten the space beneath the header and shorten the introductory paragraph to one sentence.

7. **Landing tiers / card detail — rarity terminology leaves the rule ambiguous.** “One of One,” “you found it first,” and “First find” can imply different conditions. Use one label tied to the actual rule: **Only find** for uniqueness, or **First discovery** for a permanent first-finder award.
